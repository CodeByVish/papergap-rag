Rates use the 0–1 scale. Decimal values use four decimal places. Blue bold cells mark the highest rate in each column, including exact ties. Hover over aggregate values for numerator / denominator. Pending means manual review is unfinished; N/A means unavailable. Word counts and timings are descriptive and receive no best-value highlight. Single-run timings do not establish a performance comparison.

Mean retrieved words: Mean total word count of the top-5 retrieved chunks per question (or all returned chunks if fewer than five). Overlapping text is counted repeatedly.

Corpus passages: Total number of chunks in the entire experiment corpus for this chunk size, rather than the number retrieved for one question. Smaller chunks usually produce more passages.

Corpus exhausted (questions): Number of questions for which all available chunks from the question's paper have been read but the specified word budget is still not filled. This describes exhausted available text; it does not by itself indicate retrieval failure or missing evidence.

Group labels: combined (250 questions) combines the old and new questions. new (200 questions) contains additional questions not used in the earlier parameter search and is the main validation group. regression (50 questions) reuses the reference-run questions to check reproducibility; the label does not mean a regression model or a performance decrease. New and regression questions are disjoint but may come from the same papers.

Evaluation views: top-5 evaluates the five highest-ranked chunks (or all returned chunks if fewer than five); total words vary by chunk size. 500 / 1000 / 2000 words evaluate retrieval under the respective cumulative word budgets. Higher evidence hit and complete evidence rates are better.

Primary validation metric: Complete evidence rate at 1000 words in the new group, declared before retrieval. Combined results include questions previously used for selection. Small differences do not establish a stable optimum.
